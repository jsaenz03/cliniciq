Security & Trust

How ClinicIQ secures its website, products and email, and how you can verify every claim yourself with free, independent tools.

Verified security posture

Every claim below was tested on 19 November 2026 with free, independent public scanners. We re-check regularly and publish the current results rather than a one-off badge. You can re-run each check yourself at any time: the links are in the tool list further down this page.

TLS configuration: A+ (Qualys SSL Labs)

The top grade from the industry-standard SSL Server Test. Our site uses TLS 1.2 and 1.3 only, with strong ciphers and HTTP Strict Transport Security (HSTS) with a one-year max-age, includeSubDomains and the preload directive. Certificates are issued by Let's Encrypt and renew automatically.

Security headers: A (Security Headers)

We send a full set of hardened HTTP response headers: an enforced Content-Security-Policy, X-Frame-Options DENY, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy and HSTS. The grade is capped at A because our Content-Security-Policy (correctly) allows our own inline scripts; all six header categories pass.

Safe Browsing: clean (Google)

Google Safe Browsing reports "No unsafe content found" for cliniciq.com.au and for every ClinicIQ product domain. No malware, no phishing, no unwanted software, across our main site and all five applications.

Hardened headers: B+ (Mozilla Observatory)

MDN's HTTP Observatory scores our header configuration 80/100, including the enforced Content-Security-Policy that blocks untrusted scripts, frames and connections site-wide.

Certificate issuance locked (CAA)

Both of our DNS zones publish Certificate Authority Authorization (CAA) records, so new certificates can only be issued by the certificate authorities we have approved, plus violation reports are emailed to us if anyone else tries. This is an attack surface most small businesses never close.

Certified infrastructure

This website is hosted on Netlify (SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001 certified infrastructure). Application data is hosted by Supabase (Sydney region for Docsert AI and NursEpod) and Google Cloud / Firebase (ISO 27001 certified). DNS runs on Cloudflare.

Independent verification links: SSL Labs · Security Headers · Mozilla Observatory · Google Safe Browsing. Enter cliniciq.com.au to reproduce our results.

Email you can trust

Our email is authenticated

Email sent from cliniciq.com.au is protected by all three industry-standard authentication layers: SPF, DKIM (2048-bit keys) and DMARC with reporting. If you receive an email claiming to be from us, your mail server can cryptographically verify it really came from us.

Phishing warning sign

Because of DMARC, unauthenticated mail pretending to be from cliniciq.com.au is flagged as suspicious by receiving servers. If an email pressure you to click links or share credentials "from ClinicIQ", check the sender carefully and contact us directly: real ClinicIQ email passes authentication.

Transaction email is signed

Automated email from our products (confirmations, alerts, password resets) is sent through Resend, cryptographically signed with DKIM keys published in our DNS. Your mail provider can prove every automated email is genuine.

Data, privacy and AI transparency

Privacy policy current to the December 2026 rules

Our privacy policy (version 2.1) already discloses automated decision-making under the Privacy Act amendments that commence 10 December 2026: exactly which decisions our systems make without a person, which AI outputs are always reviewed by a human, and what personal information is involved.

AI never has the final say

AI features in our products produce drafts and suggestions only: a person reviews everything before it is used, and in clinical contexts the clinician makes every clinical decision. We do not use AI to make clinical decisions or to profile individuals.

De-identified by design

Our products are built to avoid or minimise health information: PipQBoard accepts aggregate metrics only, Docsert AI accepts de-identified content only, and cIQventory is inventory-only. Patient-identifiable information is prohibited by the data rules built into each product.

All product databases host in Australia

Every ClinicIQ application database is hosted in Sydney, Australia: Docsert AI and NursEpod on Supabase (Sydney region), and cIQventory and PipQBoard on Google Cloud Firestore (Sydney, australia-southeast1). If hosting location matters for your accreditation or state requirements, contact us and we will confirm the current hosting region for the product you use.

Coordinated vulnerability disclosure

Found a security issue? Tell us.

We publish a security.txt file on every ClinicIQ property (the RFC 9116 standard researchers look for) pointing to admin@cliniciq.com.au. If you find a vulnerability, email us with the details. We ask that you give us a reasonable window to fix issues before public disclosure, and we commit to acknowledging reports promptly and keeping you informed.

Good faith is welcome here

We will not pursue legal action against genuine, good-faith security research that respects user privacy and data: no accessing or modifying data that isn't yours, no service disruption, and no public disclosure before a fix is in place.

Free cyber health tools for your practice

Cyber security matters for medical practices as much as it matters for us. These are the free, official resources we recommend to every practice manager. They are independent Australian Government and industry resources, not endorsements of ClinicIQ:

ASD Cyber Health Check

A free, anonymous five-minute self-assessment from the Australian Signals Directorate that scores your practice's cyber maturity and gives tailored actions. Start here if you do nothing else: cyber.gov.au/cyberhealthcheck

ASD Small Business Hub

Plain-English guides, the free small business cyber security handbook and action lists from the Australian Cyber Security Centre: cyber.gov.au/small-business-hub

IDCARE Small Business Cyber Resilience Service

Free one-on-one cyber resilience support for Australian small businesses (19 employees or fewer), including sole traders: idcare.org/smallbusiness

ReportCyber and Scamwatch

If your practice is hit by a cyber incident or scam, report it: ReportCyber and Scamwatch. Reporting helps protect other practices.

RACGP: Information security in general practice

The RACGP's guidance on protecting practice information covers policies, access control, backups and incident response for general practice: racgp.org.au

ADHA: Cyber security for healthcare providers

The Australian Digital Health Agency's cyber security fundamentals, self-assessment and Cyber Champions Network for healthcare providers: digitalhealth.gov.au

A note on wording you can hold us to: we self-assess against the ASD's Essential Eight Maturity Model rather than claiming certification (no organisational certification scheme exists), we never imply government endorsement or accreditation, and every security claim on this page links back to a public check you can run yourself.

Ask directly: we would rather over-explain our security than have you wonder about it.

The PIP QI Data Checklist: What to Clean Up Before the Next Quarterly Submission

By John Saenz

Registered Nurse & Founder, ClinicIQ Solutions

Your PIP QI numbers don't measure the care you deliver — they measure the care you recorded. A practice doing excellent work can still look bad on paper if smoking status is missing and blood pressures are filed as documents instead of data. Here's the monthly cleanup that fixes it.

Every quarterly PIP QI submission is a photograph of your records as they stood that day — not of the care your team actually gave. The gap between those two things is pure data quality, and it's the difference between a quality improvement program that drives change and one that just reports the same bad numbers four times a year.

Key takeaways

  • PIP QI measures de-identified data your practice extracts (typically via CAT4 or POLAR) and submits to your PHN — the numbers only move when the recording moves.
  • Run a monthly data review, not a quarterly panic: measures, gaps, and a recall worklist that actually gets worked.
  • The big three recording gaps are smoking status, blood pressure as coded data, and lapsed diabetes cycles of care.
  • Measurements in scanned documents or free text are invisible to extraction — if it isn't coded, it didn't happen.
  • PipQBoard puts your measures on one dashboard with benchmarks, year-on-year progress and focused lists to work through; free tier available.

Why the numbers lag the care

PIP QI is built on the 10 PIP QI Improvement Measures — things like patients with recorded smoking status, blood pressure management, and diabetes cycles of care. Your PHN receives de-identified extracts of your clinical data (via tools such as CAT4 or POLAR) and benchmarks your practice against the measures and your peers. The extraction can only count what's structured: a blood pressure typed into the observation fields counts; the same blood pressure inside a scanned letter does not.

That's the whole game. The practices whose PIP QI graphs trend the right way aren't necessarily doing different medicine — they've made recording structured data part of normal consultations, and they work a gap list every month instead of discovering gaps at submission time. The monthly PIP QI data checklist

Thirty minutes, once a month, owner: practice nurse or practice manager:

  1. Run the measures. Refresh your extraction tool or dashboard and look at the trend, not just the snapshot — a measure that's been flat for three months needs a plan, not another look.
  2. Smoking status. The most common easy win. Filter patients with no recorded status in the required window; add a one-line prompt to chronic disease consults so it's captured in the room.
  3. Blood pressure as data. Check that BP readings from the last 12 months are sitting in observation fields for your hypertensive and diabetic cohorts. If lots are buried in letters or notes, that's your recording-habit fix for the month.
  4. Diabetes cycles of care. Build the list of patients overdue for their annual cycle of care elements and book them — this measure rewards recall discipline more than anything else.
  5. Demographic hygiene. Dead records, duplicates and wrong dates of birth distort every denominator. Flag the obvious ones for your software's merge tools.
  6. Turn gaps into recalls. Every gap list becomes a booked worklist — nurses can fold these into existing chronic disease consults rather than creating appointments from nothing.
  7. Write down what changed. One line per month: which measure, what action, what result next month. This is also the evidence your PHN and accreditation surveyors like to see.

Quarterly submission rhythm

The submission itself should be boring: your PHN sets the schedule (typically quarterly), your extraction tool does the de-identified extract, and the practice manager submits. If the submission ever produces a surprise — a measure that moved sharply for no clinical reason — treat it as a data event to investigate (a coding change, a software update, a bulk import), because that's almost always what it is.

Frequently asked questions

What is PIP QI?

The Practice Incentives Program Quality Improvement (PIP QI) incentive supports Australian general practices to continuously improve patient care. Participating practices submit de-identified patient data to their Primary Health Network (PHN) against the 10 PIP QI Improvement Measures, and use the resulting insights to target quality improvement work.

Where does PIP QI data come from?

From your own clinical software. Data-extraction tools such as CAT4 or POLAR (Pen CS) de-identify and extract the relevant data from the practice's clinical system, and the practice submits it to its PHN on the PHN's schedule — typically quarterly.

Is patient data shared with the government under PIP QI?

The data submitted under PIP QI is de-identified — it goes to your PHN for quality improvement analysis, not to identify individual patients. Patients can be informed via the practice's privacy policy, and the extraction tools are designed so the submitted dataset cannot identify individuals.

How often should a practice review its PIP QI data?

Monthly, at minimum. Quarterly submissions are the reporting rhythm, but data quality is a monthly job: a short review of the measures, a worklist of patients with gaps (overdue BP, missing smoking status, lapsed diabetes cycle of care), and deliberate recall to close them.

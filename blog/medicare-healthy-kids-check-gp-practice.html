Medicare Healthy Kids Check: A Setup Guide for GP Practices

By John Saenz

Registered Nurse & Founder, ClinicIQ Solutions

The Medicare Healthy Kids Check went live on 1 November 2026: a once-per-lifetime developmental health check for 3-year-olds, delivered by their usual GP. No new item number — but new eligibility rules, a seven-part structure to document, and a recall list most practices have not built yet.

From 1 November 2026, Medicare funds a one-off developmental health check for every Medicare-eligible 3-year-old — the Medicare Healthy Kids Check, delivered by the child's usual GP as part of the Thriving Kids initiative. There is no new item number to learn, but there are new eligibility rules to respect, a seven-part structure to document, and a recall cohort to build. Here is what the check involves, what it pays, and how to set your practice up before the appointment requests arrive.

The timing is deliberate. The check is the primary-care front door of Thriving Kids, the Commonwealth–states program supporting children with developmental delay and neurodevelopmental differences as NDIS access rules tighten for younger children. It also revives a familiar name: Medicare ran a "Healthy Kids Check" for 4-year-olds until it was scrapped in the mid-2010s. The new version targets 3-year-olds, on the evidence that the earlier a developmental concern is identified, the more time early supports have to work (MBS Online; Department of Health).

Key takeaways

  • Live from 1 November 2026, billed through the existing time-tiered health assessment items — 701–707 for GPs, 224–227 for prescribed medical practitioners. No new item number.
  • Once per child per lifetime, between the 3rd and 4th birthday, by the child's usual GP, as a personal attendance — no telehealth.
  • Seven mandatory parts: history, examination (eyesight, hearing, oral health, growth), assessment, advice, interventions and referrals, record, and an offered written report.
  • Nurses can add information-collection time to the GP's billed tier — but examination, assessment, advice and referrals stay with the GP, and 10987/10997 cannot be claimed alongside.
  • First practical step: build the recall list (children born 2 Nov 2022 – 1 Nov 2023) and add a Healthy Kids Check reminder at the 18-month immunisation visit.

What the check is — and what it is not

The Healthy Kids Check is a targeted health assessment under a new clause 2.15.11 of the General Medical Services Table, inserted by the Health Insurance Legislation Amendment (2026 Measures No. 3) Regulations 2026. Its stated purpose is to improve early identification of developmental delay and neurodevelopmental differences, and to connect families to supports — including the new Thriving Kids services — or referral pathways (MBS Online).

Three eligibility rules do the most work in everyday practice:

  • Once per child per lifetime. A 3-year-old who has already had the check stays on ordinary attendance items for any further assessment. Ask about a prior check at booking and again at the appointment, and record the answer.
  • A defined age window. The child must be 3 on the day of service — from their 3rd birthday to the end of the day before their 4th birthday (MBS note AN.14.4).
  • The usual GP, in person. The check is delivered by the child's usual GP as a personal attendance; telehealth is not available for this service (MBS notes AN.0.36 and clause 2.15.1).

One more hook worth knowing: from 30 October 2026, the check also satisfies the Family Tax Benefit Part A health check requirement for 3-year-olds (Determination F2026L01441). Families who ask why they should book now have a practical answer.

The seven parts every check must cover

Clause 2.15.11(2) sets the minimum content, and it is a checklist rather than a vibe — each part needs to be evident in the record:

  1. History under six headings: prior presentations, admissions, medications, immunisation status and allergies; behaviours and wellbeing; developmental milestones; family, social, care and early-education context; nutrition, physical activity, screen use and sleep; toileting.
  2. Examination, including eyesight, hearing, oral health, and height and weight plotted on centile charts with BMI.
  3. Assessment — the GP's conclusions.
  4. Advice to the parent or carer.
  5. Interventions and referrals arranged as needed — optometry, audiology, dental, speech pathology, paediatric review, parenting programs.
  6. Record of all of the above in the patient's medical record.
  7. Written report with recommendations, offered to the parent or carer.

No standardised screening tool is mandated — there is no required ASQ-3 or PEDS instrument. The RACGP's AJGP guide points to practical options: the Emerging Minds CHILD framework, the Queensland Children's Hospital red-flags guide for early identification, and case-finding through the Red Book. "Parental concern about autism" is flagged as a red flag for early referral rather than something to screen out (AJGP, September 2026).

Which items to bill — and what they pay

The check rides on the existing time-tiered health assessment items. The GP claims the tier that matches the total time spent — including time contributed by a nurse or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health practitioner:

Item Tier Time Fee (100% benefit)
701BriefUnder 30 min$71.00
703Standard30–44 min$165.05
705Long45–59 min$227.75
707Prolonged60 min or more$321.75

Prescribed medical practitioners bill the parallel items 224–227 ($56.85 / $132.05 / $182.15 / $257.40). Fees are from the November 2026 MBS schedule (see the note under Sources).

Two billing mechanics matter more than the fees themselves:

  • Record actual start and finish times — the tier is claimed on total elapsed time, and communication or interpreter time counts when it is documented. Name the target group ("3-year-old health check") on every claim, and keep the documents for two years (MBS note AN.0.36).
  • Mind the sub-30-minute trap. Below 30 minutes, a standard consultation (item 36, $87.10) pays more than the Brief health assessment (item 701, $71.00) — the health assessment only wins from 30 minutes up (The Medical Republic). Book the check as a Standard or Long appointment from the start; most practices are running it as a dedicated double.

Health assessment items are already heavily bulk-billed — 99.0% in 2024–25 — and they qualify under the GP Bulk Billing Practice Incentive Program, so families can be offered the check with no out-of-pocket cost.

Where practice nurses fit — and where the boundaries sit

The practice-team model is explicitly workable: a nurse or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health practitioner can collect the history, run the developmental questions and take the measurements, and that time counts toward the GP's billed tier. What they cannot do is the assessment itself — the examination, the GP's conclusions, the advice and the referrals require the GP's personal attendance (AJGP; MBS Online).

Two rules keep the claim clean:

  • Nurse items do not stack. Items 10987 and 10997 cannot be claimed for the same service as the Healthy Kids Check. (Item 715, the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health assessment, can potentially be claimed at the same attendance where clinically relevant, subject to its own 9-month exclusion.)
  • Do not manufacture time. The PSR's August 2026 update cites a $365,000 GP repayment in which unmodified templates and undocumented nurse involvement contributed to the finding. Template fields should be completed per patient, and nurse contribution time recorded as it actually happened.

Setting up your recall list

The eligible cohort as at 1 November 2026 is children born between 2 November 2022 and 1 November 2023 — and children born late in 2022 have the least time left in their window, so start there:

  1. Run the database search. In Bp Premier: Utilities > Search > Demographics, age 3 (or the provided SQL), filtered to your active patients. Best Practice's November Data Update ships a Healthy Kids Check word-processor template alongside the search.
  2. Recall and recall-fail through your usual process. Bulk SMS works well for a one-off, well-explained check — the message can name the free check and the FTB angle.
  3. Set the standing reminder. Add a Healthy Kids Check reminder at the 18-month immunisation visit, so every child entering the cohort is flagged well before their 3rd birthday (Best Practice Software).
  4. Book the right slot. A Standard or Long appointment with the child's usual GP, with a nurse-led information-collection block beforehand if your team works that way.
  5. Screen for the lifetime rule. Ask at booking and at attendance whether the child has already had a Healthy Kids Check anywhere, and record the answer.

Recalls that go out unverified are the predictable failure mode here — a parent books, arrives, and the GP discovers the child is 4 next month with the eligibility window about to close. Prioritising the late-2022 births avoids most of that.

Getting the documentation right

Seven mandatory parts, six history headings, centile-plotted growth and a written report is a lot of capture for a 30–45 minute consult. The workable split mirrors how good practices already run health assessment documentation: the nurse's pre-consult block collects the history and measurements into the template, the GP completes examination and assessment in the consult, and the written report is generated from the record rather than typed from scratch afterwards.

Two habits keep it defensible: leave every template field patient-specific (the PSR case above was partly an unmodified-template story), and actually offer the written report — it is a mandatory part of the service, not an optional courtesy.

Where the check doesn't fit

  • Repeat or follow-up assessments. Once per lifetime means exactly that — a second developmental assessment for the same child bills under ordinary attendance items, and chronic condition management follows the usual CDM items.
  • Children outside the window. Under 3 or already 4 — the check does not apply; use routine consultations and referral pathways instead.
  • Telehealth and in-patients. Personal attendance only, and not for hospital in-patients.
  • Diagnosis. The check is an identification and referral service, not an autism or ADHD diagnostic assessment — referring early is the intended outcome, not a failure of the check.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a new Medicare item number for the Healthy Kids Check?

No. It runs through the existing time-tiered health assessment items — 701, 703, 705 and 707 for GPs, and 224–227 for prescribed medical practitioners. What is new is the eligibility layer on top: once per child per lifetime, between the 3rd and 4th birthday, delivered by the child's usual GP.

Which children are eligible for the Medicare Healthy Kids Check?

Medicare-eligible children from their 3rd birthday to the end of the day before their 4th birthday — as at 1 November 2026, children born between 2 November 2022 and 1 November 2023. One check per child per lifetime, by their usual GP, as a personal attendance. Telehealth is not available for this service.

How much does the Medicare Healthy Kids Check pay?

It pays the time-tiered health assessment rates: item 701 $71.00 (under 30 minutes), 703 $165.05 (30–44 minutes), 705 $227.75 (45–59 minutes) and 707 $321.75 (60 minutes or more), per the November 2026 MBS schedule. Below 30 minutes a standard consultation (item 36, $87.10) pays more, so most practices book the check as a Standard or Long appointment.

Can a practice nurse do part of the Healthy Kids Check?

Yes — a nurse or Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander health practitioner can collect information (history, measurements, development questions) and that time counts toward the GP's billed item tier. But the examination, assessment, advice and referrals require the GP's personal attendance, and nurse items 10987 and 10997 cannot be claimed for the same service.

How does a practice find the children who are due?

Run a database search on date of birth for the eligible cohort and recall through your usual process. Best Practice also recommends adding a Healthy Kids Check reminder at the 18-month immunisation visit, so every eligible child is flagged well before their 3rd birthday.
